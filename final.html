What · Why · How

What: monthly repository creation counts or verified canonical releases, stacked by family. Why: an overview reveals accumulation and changes in family composition before users inspect individual candidates. How: time uses horizontal position, counts use vertical position and area, and consistent family colors connect the views. Brushing links the period to feedback and development history; the exact-value table resolves small layers that are difficult to compare. Hatching identifies the incomplete acquisition month.

    Inspect a dated model to see metadata, repository links, and the meaning and source of each recorded relationship.

    Sourced relationshipRelease sequence / chronologyNode color = family · dashed chronology does not establish ancestry

    What · Why · How

    What: dated canonical models and typed, sourced relationships in family lanes. Why: development context helps users interpret benchmark differences without assuming nearby releases share ancestry. How: horizontal position represents time, vertical lanes and color identify family, and curved links represent recorded relationships. Dashed chronology links remain distinct from ancestry. Users select nodes, filter relationship types, and expand labels to inspect sources. Selected radar models stay highlighted. This uses node-link reasoning while preserving an explicit temporal axis and avoiding invented connections.

    04

    Multidimensional · comparison

    Compare capability without hiding gaps

    Eight benchmark axes, fixed published-scale normalization, and an explicit source protocol group for each dimension.

    A · solid line / circlesB · dashed line / squares

    Outer ring = the dimension’s fixed maximum. Normalized radius = (raw score − fixed minimum) / (fixed maximum − fixed minimum). No sample min–max scaling. Missing, ambiguous, out-of-range, or unmatched-setting results leave a gap. More coverage is not evidence of higher quality.

    A shared source protocol identifier is a necessary check; unknown_settings and source_table_only do not establish a controlled comparison. Inspect the raw table before drawing conclusions.

    Raw scores and evaluation settings
    Benchmark / fixed rangeComparison settingModel AModel BEvidence

    What · Why · How

    What: two model profiles across up to eight benchmark dimensions, with metadata and raw scores alongside them. Why: profile shape supports a quick reading of strengths and gaps, while the table supports precise comparison. How: fixed published ranges map each score to radius; line style and marker shape distinguish models as well as color. Missing dimensions break the profile instead of becoming zero. Per-axis protocol selection exposes mismatched settings. The radar is descriptive: polygon area is not an overall ranking, and shared source tables may leave evaluation details unspecified.

    05

    Coordinated evidence · shortlist

    Which candidates deserve a closer look?

    Inspect a cell, check its sources, and send candidates to the two-model comparison. Coverage is shown separately from capability and experience.

    B = mapped benchmark dimensions with a scoreE = rule-inferred experience reportsC = all eligible exact-model community feedbackCell shade = benchmark coverage · × = no evidence

    “Top” follows the selected sort order for the selected task. Coverage and report counts measure available evidence, not model suitability or capability. Scroll horizontally for all task columns. Click a cell, then choose A or B yourself.

    What · Why · How

    What: common tasks crossed with canonical models, showing benchmark coverage, experience reports, and attributed feedback separately. Why: users need an evidence-based shortlist before comparing capability profiles. How: aligned cells support scanning; shade encodes the fraction of mapped benchmark dimensions available, while B/E/C labels retain exact counts. Filters and sorting define the eligible set, and top-5, top-10, or custom-k controls limit the display. Cell selection reveals mapping rationale, scores, source excerpts, and gaps. Explicit A/B actions link the shortlist to the radar without inferring suitability from popularity.
