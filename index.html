01 · Introduction

From language-only systems to models that can read the visual world

This project starts with a practical question: how can a user compare models whose capabilities, scale, openness, and evaluation records differ?

What

What is an MLLM?

A multimodal large language model combines a language model with components that process other forms of information—most commonly images. It can connect visual evidence with language to answer questions about charts, documents, diagrams, photographs, or other mixed text-and-image inputs.

Why

Why model choice is difficult

New MLLMs appear rapidly, and each release may emphasize a different advantage: reasoning, OCR, parameter efficiency, openness, or a particular benchmark. A single rank hides these tradeoffs, while incompatible metrics cannot be responsibly averaged without assumptions.

Focus

What we investigate

We compare release timing, benchmark profiles, parameter scale, language and vision backbones, provider relationships, and open-weight status. Our goal is not to name one “best” model, but to support task-aware shortlisting and evidence-based exploration.

How

How visualization helps

Coordinated views move from context to comparison: an animated history establishes growth, profiles expose capability differences, scale plots reveal tradeoffs, and architecture views explain who and what sit behind each release.

Background: Wu et al., Multimodal Large Language Models: A Survey; benchmark context: Yue et al., MMMU.

What the data contains

The processed table records release date, provider, model family, open-weight flag, reported parameters, language backbone, vision encoder, and six benchmark scores: MMMU, MathVista, MMBench, MM-Vet, HallusionBench, and OCRBench.

These benchmarks cover complementary tasks, including multidisciplinary reasoning, mathematical visual reasoning, general visual question answering, hallucination-related evaluation, and optical character recognition. Scores remain separate because their scales and protocols differ.

Cleaning and safeguards

  • Dates and reported parameter counts are parsed into comparable fields.
  • Recurring provider and backbone labels are standardized.
  • Missing values remain visible; they are not guessed or imputed.
  • A release dated after the source snapshot is flagged and excluded from temporal claims.
  • “Open-weight” follows the source flag and does not imply open training data or code.

Source: OpenVLM Leaderboard, maintained with VLMEvalKit. Current local snapshot: September 17, 2025.

04 · Evaluation

What the current evidence suggests—and what we will test

Interim observations are descriptive findings from this snapshot, not claims about the entire MLLM ecosystem.

Rapid expansion

The verified records rise from 22 releases in 2023 to 171 in 2024 and 91 through the 2025 snapshot, increasing the burden of model comparison.

No single capability axis

Different leaders emerge across reasoning, OCR, hallucination-related, and general visual benchmarks; a single aggregate would hide specialization.

Scale is incomplete evidence

Reported parameters are available for 229 of 285 models. Some sub-10B models remain competitive, while many high-scoring systems omit size.

Architecture is concentrated

InternVL, LLaVA, and Qwen-VL recur across releases, making backbone and lineage views important context for apparent diversity.

What we will evaluate

Whether users can identify a suitable model under capability, openness, and parameter constraints—and explain their choice using multiple views.

How

Task-based sessions with 6–10 students or technical users, five-point ratings, open questions, and a checklist review for legibility, accessibility, missingness, and comparability.

What we will collect

Completion, time, errors, interaction paths, evidence cited, confidence, usefulness, trust in missing-data treatment, and qualitative feedback.

Success criteria

At least 80% correct completion, median confidence ≥4/5, no systematic benchmark-scale confusion, and actionable design feedback.

05 · Next steps

Strengthen the evidence, interaction, and distinct contribution

Planned—not current results

Expand and audit acquisition

Verify anomalous dates, refresh the snapshot, and manually curate missing release, license, architecture, and parameter fields from official sources.

Add responsible user evidence

Collect permissible public discussion from Reddit, X/Twitter, and model communities through approved APIs or policy-compliant manual review. Preserve provenance, remove duplicates, protect privacy, and validate text coding.

Deepen analytical rigor

Separate open-weight from fully open models, audit benchmark versions, quantify coverage bias, test frontier sensitivity, and avoid causal claims.

Build a distinctive decision aid

Develop linked task-based shortlisting, architecture lineage, profile comparison, and evidence-aware annotations—more than another leaderboard.

Complete final interactions

Add brushing/linking, provider and family filters, network zoom/pan, persistent selection, keyboard states, and responsive refinements.

Evaluate and iterate

Run the usability study, document findings, revise encodings based on evidence, and report limitations with final conclusions.
